The symptoms

Sharp lower back pain with very stiff muscles beside the spine. It hurts most when standing up or sitting, and it eases when lying face down or face up.

That pattern fits an irritated L4–L5 disc and facet joints. Sitting and standing load the lower spine, and lying down takes the load off. The stiffness is most likely protective muscle spasm: the back muscles tightening to guard the sore level.

What the images show

The level labels assume the usual five lumbar vertebrae, counted up from the sacrum. The radiologist's report may number them slightly differently. A dark L4–L5 disc is very common in people in their 40s, including people with no pain at all, so the scan points to the likely source rather than proving it.

What helps

Most flares like this settle within 1–3 weeks.

  • Keep moving gently. Walk short distances several times a day. Bed rest beyond a day or two tends to make it worse.
  • Heat on the lower back for 15–20 minutes at a time to relax the spasm.
  • An anti-inflammatory like ibuprofen or naproxen at the label dose for a few days, if your health allows it.
  • Gentle prone extension. Lie face down, then progress to propping on your elbows for 1–2 minutes. Stop if pain spreads into a buttock or leg.
  • Break up sitting every 20–30 minutes and use lumbar support. Get up from a chair by sliding to the edge and pushing up with your arms.
  • Avoid for now: heavy lifting, bending while twisting, and long drives.
  • See a physical therapist if it isn't clearly better in a week or two. Core and hip strengthening is the long-term fix.

Get seen urgently if any of these appear: numbness in the groin or saddle area, new trouble controlling the bladder or bowels, weakness in a leg or foot, or a fever along with the back pain.
